📜 History 3: The Great Disappointment of 1844

Introduction

The Great Disappointment is one of the most important events in Adventist history. Thousands of believers expected Jesus Christ to return on October 22, 1844, based on their understanding of Bible prophecy.

The Expectation

William Miller and his followers believed that the prophecy of Daniel 8:14 pointed to the Second Coming of Christ. As the date approached, many believers prayed, studied the Bible, and prepared for Jesus' return.

The Disappointment

When October 22, 1844 passed and Jesus did not return, believers experienced deep sorrow and confusion. Many people abandoned the movement, while others continued searching the Scriptures for answers.

A New Understanding

Through prayer and Bible study, some Advent believers discovered that the prophecy was correct, but the event was misunderstood. They came to understand that Jesus had entered the Most Holy Place in the heavenly sanctuary to begin a special phase of His ministry.

A Foundation for the SDA Church

This experience led to deeper Bible study and eventually contributed to the formation of the Seventh-day Adventist Church. The Great Disappointment taught believers the importance of trusting God's Word even when they do not fully understand His plans.
