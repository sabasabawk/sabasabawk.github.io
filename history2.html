SDA History Lesson 2

William Miller and the Millerite Movement

Who Was William Miller?

William Miller was an American farmer, military veteran, and Bible student. After years of careful Bible study, he became convinced that the prophecies of Daniel revealed that Jesus Christ would soon return.

Using the Bible as his guide, Miller compared Scripture with Scripture and developed a strong interest in prophecy.

The Prophecy of Daniel 8:14

William Miller focused on Daniel 8:14:

"Unto two thousand and three hundred days; then shall the sanctuary be cleansed."

Applying the prophetic day-year principle, he concluded that the 2300 prophetic days represented 2300 literal years.

The Millerite Movement

Beginning in the 1830s, Miller preached throughout the United States. Thousands accepted the message that Christ's return was near.

People from many Christian denominations joined the Advent Movement. These believers became known as Millerites.

  • Large evangelistic meetings were held.
  • Bible prophecy was widely studied.
  • Thousands prepared for Christ's return.
  • The movement spread across North America and beyond.

Expectation of Christ's Return

The Millerites eventually believed that Jesus would return on October 22, 1844. Believers prayed, studied, and eagerly waited for the Second Coming.

However, Christ did not return on that date. This event became known as "The Great Disappointment."

Importance to SDA History

Although Miller's understanding of the sanctuary was incomplete, the Advent Movement laid the foundation for the Seventh-day Adventist Church.

Many believers continued studying the Bible after 1844 and discovered important truths about Christ's ministry in the heavenly sanctuary, the Sabbath, and the Three Angels' Messages.

Key Facts

  • William Miller lived from 1782 to 1849.
  • He preached the soon return of Jesus.
  • The Millerite Movement spread rapidly.
  • October 22, 1844 became a pivotal date in Adventist history.
  • The movement led to the birth of the Seventh-day Adventist Church.
