🙏 Lesson 5: Prayer and Fasting

Key Text

"Howbeit this kind goeth not out but by prayer and fasting."

Matthew 17:21

Introduction

Prayer and fasting are powerful spiritual practices that draw believers closer to God. Throughout the Bible, God's people combined prayer with fasting during times of repentance, spiritual need, guidance, and special dedication.

Fasting is not a way to earn God's favor. Instead, it is a voluntary act of self-denial that helps us focus more fully on God and His will.

What Is Fasting?

Fasting usually means abstaining from food for a period of time in order to devote oneself to prayer and spiritual reflection. In some situations, people may fast from certain foods or other activities that distract them from seeking God.

The purpose of fasting is not to impress others but to strengthen our relationship with God.

Biblical Examples of Fasting

  • Moses fasted while receiving God's law.
  • Esther called for a fast before approaching the king.
  • Daniel practiced fasting and prayer.
  • Jesus fasted for forty days in the wilderness.
  • The early church fasted when seeking God's guidance.

Benefits of Prayer and Fasting

  • Deepens spiritual commitment.
  • Helps focus on God.
  • Strengthens faith.
  • Encourages humility.
  • Provides clarity when making decisions.
  • Draws believers closer to God.

Proper Attitude in Fasting

Jesus taught that fasting should be done sincerely and privately, not for public recognition. God looks at the heart and desires genuine devotion rather than outward display.

Fasting should always be accompanied by prayer, Bible study, repentance, and a desire to follow God's will.

When Should Christians Fast?

Christians may choose to fast during times of:

  • Personal spiritual renewal.
  • Seeking God's guidance.
  • Special prayer requests.
  • Repentance and confession.
  • Interceding for others.
  • Church revival and mission.
