🌍 History 11: SDA Church in Africa

Introduction

The Seventh-day Adventist Church entered Africa in the late 19th century through missionary efforts. The message of the gospel quickly spread across many regions of the continent.

Early Mission Work

One of the earliest Adventist mission stations in Africa was established in South Africa in the 1890s. From there, the message expanded into East Africa, West Africa, and Central Africa.

Arrival in East Africa

Adventist missionaries reached East Africa in the early 1900s. Countries like Kenya, Tanzania, Uganda, and others became important centers for church growth and evangelism.

Education and Healthcare

The church established schools, hospitals, and training centers across Africa. These institutions helped improve education, healthcare, and community development while sharing the gospel message.

Growth of the Church

Africa has become one of the fastest-growing regions for the Seventh-day Adventist Church. Millions of believers now worship in thousands of churches across the continent.

Mission Today

African Adventists continue to play a major role in global mission work, sending missionaries, pastors, and students to other parts of the world.
