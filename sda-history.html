History of the Seventh-day Adventist Church

The Seventh-day Adventist Church began in the 1840s in the United States following a religious revival movement led by William Miller.

When Jesus did not return as expected on October 22, 1844, the event became known as the Great Disappointment. After this, believers continued studying the Bible and discovered important truths including the seventh-day Sabbath.

Key pioneers included Ellen G. White, James White, and Joseph Bates. Through Bible study, prayer, and evangelism, they helped establish the church.

In 1863, the Seventh-day Adventist Church was officially organized and has since grown into a worldwide Christian movement.

Core Beliefs

  • The Bible is God's inspired Word.
  • Salvation comes through Jesus Christ.
  • The seventh-day Sabbath is God's holy day.
  • Jesus Christ will return soon.
  • Christians should live healthy lives and share the gospel.
