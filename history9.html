🎓 History 9: Adventist Education System

Introduction

Education has always been an important part of the mission of the Seventh-day Adventist Church. Adventists believe that true education helps students develop mentally, physically, socially, and spiritually.

The Beginning of Adventist Education

In the 1870s, church leaders recognized the need for schools that would provide both academic learning and Christian values. They wanted young people to be prepared for service to God and humanity.

Battle Creek College

In 1874, Battle Creek College was established in Michigan, USA. It was the first official Seventh-day Adventist institution of higher learning and became a model for future Adventist schools.

Ellen G. White's Influence

Ellen G. White strongly supported Christian education. She taught that education should prepare students not only for careers but also for faithful service and character development.

Growth Around the World

As the church expanded globally, Adventist schools were established in many countries. Today, the Adventist education system includes primary schools, secondary schools, colleges, and universities.

Education Today

The Seventh-day Adventist Church operates one of the largest Protestant education systems in the world. Millions of students study in Adventist institutions each year, receiving both academic and spiritual training.
