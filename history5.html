📜 History 5: Ellen G. White and the Prophetic Gift

Introduction

Ellen G. White was one of the founders of the Seventh-day Adventist Church. Adventists believe that God used her ministry to guide, encourage, and strengthen the church through the spiritual gift of prophecy.

Her Early Life

Ellen Gould Harmon was born on November 26, 1827, in Maine, United States. As a young girl she accepted Jesus Christ and later became involved in the Advent Movement that followed the preaching of William Miller.

The First Vision

In December 1844, shortly after the Great Disappointment, Ellen received her first vision. The vision encouraged disappointed believers to keep their faith in God and continue studying the Bible.

Her Ministry

Throughout her life, Ellen G. White traveled, preached, wrote books, and shared messages she believed were given to her by God. Her writings covered topics such as Christian living, education, health, evangelism, and Bible prophecy.

Her Books

Ellen White wrote many influential books, including "Steps to Christ," "The Desire of Ages," "The Great Controversy," and "Education." These books continue to inspire millions of readers around the world.

Her Legacy

Ellen G. White died in 1915, but her writings remain an important part of Adventist history. Adventists regard her writings as a source of counsel that points people back to the Bible.
