SDA History Lesson 4

Discovery of the Seventh-day Sabbath

After the Great Disappointment

Following the Great Disappointment of 1844, a group of Advent believers continued searching the Scriptures for truth. As they studied the Bible, they became convinced that God had never changed His holy Sabbath.

They discovered that the seventh day, Saturday, remained God's biblical day of worship according to the Fourth Commandment.

Rachel Oakes Preston

A Seventh Day Baptist named Rachel Oakes Preston played an important role in introducing the Sabbath truth to Advent believers.

She shared biblical evidence that the seventh-day Sabbath was still binding upon Christians.

Joseph Bates and the Sabbath Message

Joseph Bates, a retired sea captain and Advent preacher, accepted the Sabbath truth after careful Bible study.

He became one of the strongest advocates of Sabbath observance and wrote books and articles explaining its importance.

His influence helped many early Advent believers embrace the Sabbath.

Biblical Foundation

Early Adventists found strong biblical support for Sabbath observance:

  • Genesis 2:1-3 – God blessed and sanctified the seventh day.
  • Exodus 20:8-11 – The Fourth Commandment.
  • Isaiah 58:13-14 – Blessings of honoring the Sabbath.
  • Luke 4:16 – Jesus kept the Sabbath.
  • Revelation 14:12 – God's end-time people keep His commandments.

Acceptance Among Early Adventists

James White, Ellen White, and many other Advent believers accepted the Sabbath message through Bible study.

The Sabbath soon became one of the defining beliefs of the developing Advent movement.

Importance to SDA History

The discovery of the Sabbath helped shape the identity of the Seventh-day Adventist Church.

Today, Sabbath observance remains one of the church's most distinctive and important teachings, emphasizing loyalty to God as Creator and Redeemer.
