📜 History 4: Discovery of the Seventh-day Sabbath

Introduction

After the Great Disappointment of 1844, Advent believers continued studying the Bible carefully. As they searched the Scriptures, they discovered important truths that would become foundational teachings of the Seventh-day Adventist Church.

The Biblical Sabbath

One of these truths was the seventh-day Sabbath. Early Advent believers found that the fourth commandment teaches the observance of the seventh day of the week, Saturday, as God's holy day of rest and worship.

Rachel Oakes Preston

A Seventh Day Baptist named Rachel Oakes Preston shared the Sabbath truth with Advent believers. Her influence helped lead several Adventist leaders to study the subject more deeply.

Joseph Bates

Joseph Bates became one of the strongest supporters of the Sabbath. After studying the Bible, he accepted the seventh-day Sabbath and wrote books explaining its importance. His work helped spread the message among Advent believers.

A Distinctive Belief

James White, Ellen G. White, and many other early Adventists accepted the Sabbath truth. It became one of the defining beliefs of the Seventh-day Adventist Church and remains important to Adventists today.
