📜 History 6: Organization of the SDA Church

Introduction

As the Advent Movement grew after 1844, believers realized that they needed a proper organization to support evangelism, manage church property, publish literature, and coordinate mission work.

The Need for Organization

In the early years, Advent believers operated without a formal church structure. As membership increased, challenges arose concerning church leadership, finances, and ownership of church buildings and publishing facilities.

Choosing a Name

In 1860, church leaders selected the name "Seventh-day Adventist." The name reflected two important beliefs: observance of the seventh-day Sabbath and faith in the soon return (Advent) of Jesus Christ.

Official Organization

On May 21, 1863, the Seventh-day Adventist Church was officially organized in Battle Creek, Michigan, USA. The newly formed church began with a small membership but had a strong commitment to Bible truth and mission work.

The General Conference

The General Conference was established to unite churches and coordinate the worldwide work of the denomination. This structure helped support evangelism, education, healthcare, and global mission activities.

Growth and Mission

Through organization, the church was able to expand its publishing work, send missionaries to new countries, establish schools and hospitals, and spread the gospel around the world.
