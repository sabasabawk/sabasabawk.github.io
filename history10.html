🌍 History 10: Worldwide Mission Expansion

Introduction

From its small beginnings in North America, the Seventh-day Adventist Church developed a strong commitment to sharing the gospel with the entire world. Mission became the heart of the church's identity.

The First Overseas Missionary

In 1874, J. N. Andrews was sent to Switzerland as the first official Seventh-day Adventist missionary. This marked the beginning of organized international mission work.

Expansion into New Continents

Following Andrews' mission, Adventist missionaries traveled to Europe, Africa, Asia, Australia, South America, and many islands of the Pacific. They established churches, schools, hospitals, and publishing houses.

Publishing and Evangelism

Printed literature played a major role in spreading the Adventist message. Books, magazines, and tracts were translated into many languages, helping reach people around the world.

Medical and Educational Missions

Missionaries not only preached the gospel but also established schools, clinics, and hospitals. These institutions helped meet people's physical, educational, and spiritual needs.

A Global Church

Today, the Seventh-day Adventist Church is present in nearly every country of the world. Millions of members worship in thousands of churches, continuing the mission of sharing the Three Angels' Messages and preparing people for the return of Jesus Christ.
