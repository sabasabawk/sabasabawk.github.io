SDA History Lesson 10

Worldwide Mission Expansion

Introduction

After the organization of the Seventh-day Adventist Church in 1863, the movement quickly developed a strong missionary focus. Early Adventists believed that the Gospel must be shared with the entire world before the second coming of Jesus Christ.

The Great Commission

The mission of the church is based on Jesus’ command:

“Go ye therefore, and teach all nations…” (Matthew 28:19-20)

This inspired Adventists to take the message beyond the United States and into other continents.

First Overseas Missionary

In 1874, the Seventh-day Adventist Church sent its first official overseas missionary, J. N. Andrews, to Switzerland.

This marked the beginning of global Adventist mission work.

Rapid Global Expansion

  • Europe – Switzerland, England, Germany, Scandinavia
  • Africa – Early missions in the late 1800s
  • Asia – India, China, Japan, Philippines
  • South America – Brazil, Argentina, Peru
  • Pacific Islands – Papua New Guinea and surrounding regions

Role of Institutions in Mission

Mission work expanded through:

  • Schools and colleges
  • Hospitals and clinics
  • Publishing houses
  • Radio and media ministries
  • Literature evangelism

Challenges Faced by Missionaries

  • Language barriers
  • Tropical diseases
  • Travel difficulties
  • Opposition to the Gospel
  • Financial limitations

Despite these challenges, missionaries remained faithful and committed.

Growth Into a Global Church

From a small group in North America, the Seventh-day Adventist Church has grown into a worldwide movement with members in almost every country.

Today, the church operates thousands of schools, hospitals, and mission projects globally.

Importance to SDA History

Worldwide mission expansion shows the fulfillment of God’s plan to spread the everlasting Gospel to every nation, tribe, and language.
