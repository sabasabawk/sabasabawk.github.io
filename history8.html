🏥 History 8: Medical Missionary Work

Introduction

Medical missionary work became an important part of the Seventh-day Adventist Church's mission. Adventists believe that Jesus cared for both the physical and spiritual needs of people, and the church seeks to follow His example.

The Health Message

Through Bible study and the counsel given through Ellen G. White, Adventists came to understand the importance of healthy living. They promoted proper nutrition, exercise, cleanliness, fresh air, rest, and trust in God.

The First Health Institution

In 1866, Adventists established the Western Health Reform Institute in Battle Creek, Michigan. This institution later became known as the Battle Creek Sanitarium.

Dr. John Harvey Kellogg

Dr. John Harvey Kellogg served as a physician and helped develop the Battle Creek Sanitarium into a well-known medical center. He promoted health education and preventive medicine.

Medical Mission Around the World

As the church expanded globally, hospitals, clinics, and health centers were established in many countries. These institutions provided medical care while sharing the love of Christ with patients.

Medical Ministry Today

Today, the Seventh-day Adventist Church operates one of the largest Protestant healthcare systems in the world. Adventist hospitals, clinics, and healthcare professionals continue serving millions of people each year.
